I still remember the first year or two after graduation, when I felt my work had hit a ceiling.

It wasn't that the work was hard. It was that most of my daily tasks had become familiar, almost effortless, and I gradually felt there was no challenge left.

So I thought about taking some orders on Xianyu. On one hand I could earn a little extra money; on the other, I wanted to touch things outside of my day job.

Back then I only knew database development, so the title and description of my listing stuck to what I was comfortable with: SQL development, Oracle, PLSQL.

But real market demand is never that narrow.

Some people wanted a website built. Some needed data problems solved. Some asked me to help with coursework and assignments. There were all kinds of requests I had never touched before.

I was pretty reckless back then, ha. Whatever people asked, my first reaction was to say I could do it, and then tell them, "I'm at work right now, I'll get back to you after hours."

In reality I was sneaking off to Bilibili during the day, searching for tutorials and learning on the spot.

General-purpose AI wasn't as convenient as it is now. The first time I used ChatGPT was December 2022.

If I could figure something out within a day and felt confident I could deliver it, I let the client place the order and pay. If I still hadn't understood it after a full day, I had to make an excuse: work has been busy lately, I can't take this one for now.

It's funny thinking about it now.

And my prices were really cheap. Ten yuan, a few dozen yuan, I took it all. There was no business logic behind the quotes, just the thought that "I get to learn something anyway, and I might earn a bit too."

Plainly put, I was cheap labor, ha.

---

But looking back, those one or two years of taking orders gave me far more than the money.

First, the way I learned changed a lot.

Before that, I would learn a technology first and then think about when I might use it. Taking orders reversed the order completely: I ran into a real need first, then figured out how to solve it.

If I didn't understand something, I looked it up, watched tutorials, and tried it myself until the problem was solved and delivered.

**It was a very short learning loop.**

And clients' needs were usually outside my comfort zone, with no ready-made standard answer. After enough of them, I slowly realized that many things that look hard aren't as hard as they seem, as long as you're willing to spend the time.

That experience gave me something important: **the confidence to face unknown problems.**

There was another thing I didn't notice at the time.

Many clients can't articulate their own needs at all.

They may only know what problem they hit, or what effect they want, but how to do it, what technology it needs, and even what they actually want in the end, may not be something they can express clearly.

So I had to talk with them bit by bit, help them sort out the requirements, and sometimes offer suggestions and solutions in return.

Only after doing that many times did I gradually develop **the ability to understand vague requirements**.

Later I realized this experience also gave me a habit: **letting market demand drive my learning.**

I don't learn whatever technology is trending. I study the technology and the solution behind a problem only when someone actually has that problem and is willing to pay to get it solved.

And so I took orders for a year or two.

At the time I thought I was just earning some extra money and learning a bit of technology along the way. I never imagined how much those experiences would help my later work.

Only as my years of experience grew and the business I touched became more complex did I gradually see that what benefited me most wasn't the specific technologies I learned back then.

It was the ability to learn and get up to speed quickly in unfamiliar territory; the willingness to try when things get hard; and the patience to untangle vague business requirements piece by piece before offering my own suggestions and solutions.

Many of those abilities were built slowly, one ten-yuan or fifty-yuan order at a time.

Now, scrolling back through these Xianyu order records, I suddenly feel quite emotional.

I really had no money and no resources back then. If I wanted to earn a little more, the only way was to learn a little more and do a little more myself.

**Maybe that's the gift of being young and poor.**
